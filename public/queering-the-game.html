Welcome to the "Queering the Game" Workshop

This is an interactive, rhizomatic journey through the 4 pillars of decolonial machinima creation: OBSERVE, FEEL, HACK, and CO-CREATE.

Portal A — The Anti-Natural Museum

The word "natural" has historically been used as a moral and political weapon against queer bodies and non-binary ecologies. You are standing in the foyer of the Anti-Natural Museum, a digital space designed specifically to dismantle the logic of victory and extraction.

Here, we do not play to win. As Bonnie Ruberg argues in Video Games Have Always Been Queer, queerness is found in the practice of playing itself: the failure, the slowness, the refusal of the victory state. Look at games like Dys4ia by Anna Anthropy and Lim by merritt k.

📚 References & Hyperlinks

All games, theorists, and archives referenced in this workshop:

Games

  • Dys4ia — Anna Anthropy (2012). An autobiographical game about hormonal transition.
  • Lim — merritt k (2012). A game about the violent experience of inhabiting a liminal social space.
  • Tchia — Awaceb (2023). An open-world adventure inspired by New Caledonia.
  • When Rivers Were Trails — Indian Land Tenure Foundation / Elizabeth LaPensée (2019). A point-and-click adventure about the impact of colonization on Indigenous communities.
  • Tell Me Why — DONTNOD Entertainment (2020). An episodic adventure featuring a transgender protagonist.
  • If Found... — Dreamfeel (2020). A visual novel about going home, coming out, and erasing everything.
  • Third World: The Bottom Dimension — Gabriel Massan / Serpentine Arts Technologies (2022–ongoing). A machinima exploring Black Brazilian experience through colonialism and extraction.

Theorists & Writers

  • Bonnie Ruberg — Video Games Have Always Been Queer (NYU Press, 2019). Also: Queer Game Studies (2017, co-edited with Adrienne Shaw).
  • Jack (Judith) Halberstam — The Queer Art of Failure (Duke University Press, 2011). "Failure is an alternative to capitalist, heteronormative logics of success."
  • Antônio Bispo dos Santos (Nêgo Bispo) — A terra dá, a terra quer (The Land Gives, the Land Wants). Quilombola thought and indigenous epistemologies.

Archives & Festivals

  • Mix Brasil Festival — The largest LGBTQIA+ cultural festival in Latin America since 1993. Includes Mix.XR program for immersive queer experiences.
  • Serpentine Galleries — Commissioning body for Third World: The Bottom Dimension.
